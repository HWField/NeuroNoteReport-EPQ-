\subsection{Conversations with users}
To dive further into the user experience, I conducted informal conversations with several users. The discussions focused on their overall impressions, specific features they found useful, and any areas for improvement. Please find below a list of notes I made from each interview:

\subsubsection*{User 1:}
Use 1 is a peer / friend of mine attending university currently. They were on a video call with me as they tried the software. The user expressed that the platform felt intuitive and easy to navigate. They particularly liked the ability to switch between text and whiteboard modes without losing context. 

Their main use case for the note-taking software is to record thoughts and ideas during university lectures which they feel this works well for however, they suggested that having an integrated system to transcribe an audio file into a note (e.g. from a lecturer speaking) and summarise / nicely format in my styling could be an amazing feature for further optimising user efficiency.

This is definitely a feature I can look to implement, after the EPQ program has concluded I definitely aim to continue development of the platform and will add this to my research list (\nameref{Planning:PostEPQPlan}).

He has continued to use my platform since our call and has now recorded over 50 notes!
\subsubsection*{User 2:}
User 2 is a business consultant who used the app for the first time whilst sitting down with me. They found the interactive tutorial very useful to quickly learn the platform and appreciated the help hints placed throughout the UI. They did however express a want for greater text/written instruction manuals that could they could read to further understand the more complex tools such as the maths blocks within notes.

They thought that the project would be very useful for their line of work as they could "weave together their notes from different projects in a succinct way"

\subsubsection*{User 3:}
User 3 is an Executive officer of a financial services organization who had a self-guided experience of the app. They appreciated the mathematics mode for its simple access to the vast array of tools the type-setting interface provides. They were glad I didn't "reinvent the wheel" and that the system was resultingly "simple to use, much more so then word which is inefficient as it is too reliant on clicking around". They also liked the whiteboard mode for its ability to freely record information in a huge (infinite) space

\subsection*{Reviewing Feedback Forms and Surveys:}

When building my project I made sure to include an easily accessible feedback form within the application itself so that users who didn't personally know me (and thus may miss my communications) could still have their voices heard. This form was designed to be quick and easy to fill out, allowing users to provide their thoughts and suggestions without interrupting their workflow. The feedback form included questions about usability, feature requests, and overall satisfaction. 

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Evaluation/PlatformEvaluation/media/feedback.png}
    \caption{Feedback form data}
\end{figure}

The above image shows the numerical responses I received from users who filled out the feedback form. In total, I received 51 form submissions. The average rating across all categories was 4 out of 5, indicating a high level of satisfaction within the platform.

Whilst the feedback was overall very positive, I did receive some constructive criticism. A few users mentioned that they found the initial setup process a bit confusing and suggested that an onboarding tutorial could help new users get started more easily. I was slightly confused by this comment as I had spent a lot of time designing and implementing an interactive tour, however, it turns out that in some scenarios (if a user signs up but leaves straight away, thus tricking the tutorial into thinking it has been completed) the tutorial does not appear again unless manually summoned.  I will look to fix this bug in future versions of the platforms as I can appreciate this would be frustrating for new users.

A few users also requested more advanced features, such as integration with other productivity tools (e.g., calendar apps, task managers) and enhanced collaboration capabilities for team projects. These suggestions align with my initial vision for the platform, and I plan to explore these features in future updates however (as identified in my project choice and planning) my primary focus for this project was to create a solid foundation for note-taking and organization, which I believe I have successfully achieved.

Additionally, some users expressed a desire for more customization options, such as the ability to change themes or layouts to better suit their personal preferences. This is an area I can easily expand upon in future versions of the platform, as I have already laid the groundwork for a modular design that can accommodate such changes. 

Overall, the feedback I received through the form was invaluable in helping me understand how users interact with my platform and what improvements can be made. I am grateful for the time and effort users took to provide their insights, and I am committed to using this feedback to enhance the user experience in future iterations of the project.


\subsection*{Back to My Work Experience:}
The origins of my project started from a two-week placement at a software development company in Cambridge and towards the end of my project I had the opportunity to return to this company for a month long summer internship. 

During this time I was able to show my project to a number of employees (technical developers, business development staff and operations leaders) and receive their feedback. They were all very impressed with the platform I have created, and I received a lot of positive feedback. Their Head of Operations in particular commented that my use of a tag and graph system seems so obviously logical now and that he just wishes a more commercial option had this feature (Maybe this project will be that option one day!). Particularly rewarding to hear was a developer ask when my code would be open-sourced as they would love to get stuck in and possibly even use my platform for their daily notes - high praise indeed.

It was also reinforcing to learn that many of the technical decisions I have made since my first encounter with the company (such as my use of Tiptap for my rich text editor) were also recently selected by the team with a very similar rationale to myself. This demonstrates that throughout this project I have developed my key analysis and comparison skills to a high level which will serve me well throughout my further education and career.
